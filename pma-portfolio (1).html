Most scripts get written backwards — a hook first, facts fitted around it later. We investigate the evidence, identify the strongest story angle the evidence actually supports, verify the facts that carry the argument, and only then build the narrative.

The order matters. A script built on unverified numbers collapses the moment a viewer checks a source. A script built on research holds up in the comments section — which, for a channel covering business and geopolitics, is where credibility is actually won or lost.

INDEPENDENT PORTFOLIO RESEARCH — SAMPLE 01

India's Semiconductor Bet

Technology / Business / Geopolitics

Research question: Can a country with no prior fabrication industry build a competitive chip-making base in under a decade — and is India's first fab actually the leap it's being sold as?

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

India's flagship bet is a ₹91,000 crore (~$11B) fabrication plant in Dholera, Gujarat, built by Tata Electronics with Taiwan's Powerchip Semiconductor Manufacturing Corporation (PSMC). It's India's first front-end fab — the step no Indian company had attempted before, distinct from the assembly-and-packaging plants already running.
By April 2026 the project was reported roughly 50% complete, with trial production targeted for December 2026. But the more interesting research finding isn't the fab itself — it's a live disagreement in the coverage about which chip generation it will actually start on.

CONTRADICTORY CLAIMS — FLAGGED IN RESEARCH

PSMC's own chairman stated at announcement that Dholera would start on the 28nm node and later move to 22nm. A Bloomberg report cited by Business Standard in July 2026 said commercial production would instead begin on the older, "mature" 90nm node, with 28nm framed as a later step. Both claims are on the record from credible outlets — the gap between them is itself the story: is this a leading-edge fab, or a deliberately conservative first step that lets India learn the harder process later? PMA's brief would open with that tension rather than hide it.

STORY ANGLE

The public narrative is "India enters the chip race." The research suggests a more precise and more interesting angle: India is choosing to master the boring, high-volume end of chip-making first — the end China and Taiwan built their industries on decades ago — rather than chasing the 3nm race it cannot win yet. That's a strategy story, not a milestone story.

GLOBAL CONTEXT

India's semiconductor demand is projected by Tata Sons chairman N Chandrasekaran to cross $110B by 2030, inside a global industry forecast to reach $1 trillion the same year. Dholera's planned capacity — 50,000 wafers a month, an estimated 3 billion chips a year — is real scale, but it's an assembly-and-fabrication entrant competing against Taiwan's TSMC and South Korea's Samsung, both decades ahead on process node.

SHORT SCRIPT EXCERPT

Everyone covering this story keeps calling it India's "chip moment." Almost nobody is asking the more interesting question: why is India's first fab starting on technology the industry perfected twenty years ago?

That's not a failure. It might be the smartest part of the plan.

INDEPENDENT PORTFOLIO RESEARCH — SAMPLE 02

The Battle for India's Quick Commerce Market

Business / Startups / Technology

Research question: Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart and Amazon are all burning cash to deliver groceries in ten minutes — so which parts of this business are actually becoming a real business, and which are still a subsidy war?

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

India's quick commerce category was valued near $11.5B (₹95,500 crore) at the end of 2025 and is growing over 70% year on year, according to Datum Intelligence data cited by Reuters. Blinkit (owned by Eternal, formerly Zomato) holds a commanding lead — reported between 46% and 50% market share depending on the tracker — with Swiggy Instamart and Zepto contesting a close second.
The more useful finding sits underneath the share numbers: Blinkit has started reporting positive adjusted EBITDA in consecutive quarters of FY26, which changes the framing from "who is growing fastest" to "who is close to running this as an actual business."

CONTRADICTORY CLAIMS — FLAGGED IN RESEARCH

Store counts for the same month diverge by tracker: one industry aggregator put Blinkit at 1,954 stores in March 2026, while Blinkit's own reporting (via Eternal's filings, cited by Upstox) put it at 2,243 as of March 31, 2026. Swiggy Instamart's own store count also shifts between reports — 1,038 in one March 2026 snapshot, 1,143 in a May 2026 filing-based figure, reflecting fast month-to-month expansion rather than an error. A script script built on this data has to state which figure comes from a company filing versus a third-party scrape — they answer different questions.

STORY ANGLE

The consumer story is "who delivers fastest." The business story — the one with actual stakes — is that India's government reportedly told all three platforms in January 2026 to stop marketing themselves as "10-minute" delivery over rider-safety concerns, while a retailer body separately asked the competition regulator to investigate predatory pricing. The real turning point in this category isn't a funding round. It's regulation catching up to the promise that built the category.

Unit economics, not funding size, now separate the leader from the field. Blinkit's shift to adjusted EBITDA profitability — even a thin ₹4 crore, then ₹37 crore across two quarters — is a more consequential data point for a business audience than any single funding headline, because it's the first hard evidence that dense dark-store networks can become profitable at scale in India, not just in theory.

Insight: The quick commerce war stopped being about who can burn cash the longest. It's now about who built store density before the money ran out.

Angle: This isn't a growth story anymore. It's a story about which competitor built real infrastructure while everyone was watching the discount war.

The AI Search War

Technology / Business

Research question: Perplexity is valued in the tens of billions and growing fast — so is it actually taking search share from Google, or winning a different, smaller game?

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Perplexity's valuation climbed from roughly $121M in March 2023 to a reported $22.6–23B by January 2026 — with talks of a round above $30B reported in August 2026, though unconfirmed by the company. Query volume grew from about 230 million a month in mid-2024 to an estimated 1.2–1.5 billion a month by mid-2026.
Set against that: Statcounter's own May 2026 data still puts Google at over 90% of global search market share. Both facts are true at once, and the research finding is about what that gap actually means.

CONTRADICTORY CLAIMS — FLAGGED IN RESEARCH

User-count reporting on Perplexity is genuinely inconsistent across trackers as of mid-2026: standard web/app analytics put monthly active users around 34–45 million, while Sacra's broader estimate — which folds in the Comet browser and API usage — puts total reach above 100 million. Neither number is wrong; they're measuring different things. A script that quotes "Perplexity has 100 million users" without that qualifier is repeating a marketing framing, not a verified fact.

STORY ANGLE

This isn't "Perplexity vs Google" — that framing doesn't survive the market-share data. The real story is distribution: Perplexity's growth since late 2025 tracks almost exactly with product moves — a free Comet browser (October 2025), a Samsung Galaxy S26 preinstall deal, an Airtel India partnership, and a February 2026 pivot to drop advertising entirely while OpenAI added ads to ChatGPT the same month. The company isn't beating Google on search. It's betting that "no ads, cited answers" becomes a wedge into daily browsing habits before Google's own AI Mode closes the gap.

COMPETITIVE FIELD

Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode reach an estimated two billion monthly users on distribution alone. OpenAI's ChatGPT has crossed roughly one billion monthly active users. Anthropic, by contrast, has stayed out of consumer search almost entirely — its reported growth (one tracker cites annualized revenue crossing roughly $30B by April 2026, driven heavily by Claude Code) comes from enterprise and developer tools, not search. That's a useful contrast for a script: three different companies, three different bets on where AI actually intercepts a user's daily habits.

SHORT SCRIPT EXCERPT

Perplexity is worth more than twenty billion dollars. Google still answers more than nine out of ten search queries on Earth. Both of those numbers are true, and if you only read the funding headlines, you'd never guess the second one.

INDEPENDENT SCRIPT SAMPLE

Why India's Quick Commerce War Is Different From What You Think

≈800 words · full script preview

HOOK
What looks like a race to deliver groceries faster is actually a much bigger battle — and the ten-minute promise you keep seeing in the ads might already be a problem for the people running it.

OPEN QUESTION
Three companies — Blinkit, Zepto, and Swiggy Instamart — are spending billions of rupees to get a bag of onions to your door before the delivery app even finishes loading. The question worth asking isn't who's fastest. It's whether any of them can actually make money doing this — and what happens now that India's government has started paying attention to how they get it done.

CONTEXT
Quick commerce in India crossed roughly eleven and a half billion dollars in value by the end of 2025, growing faster than almost any retail category in the country. Blinkit — owned by Eternal, the company that used to be Zomato — built the largest network, reportedly north of two thousand dark stores by March 2026. Zepto and Swiggy Instamart are close behind, each running over a thousand of their own. These aren't warehouses in the traditional sense. They're small, densely packed micro-fulfilment centers, built specifically so a rider never has more than a few minutes of road between the shelf and your door.

FIRST REVELATION
Here's what most coverage of this story misses: for the first time, one of these companies is actually making money. Blinkit posted positive adjusted EBITDA in two consecutive quarters through FY26 — a small number, a few crore rupees, but a real one. That matters more than it sounds, because until recently, "profitable quick commerce" was treated as a contradiction in terms.

DATA
The numbers tell a lopsided story. Blinkit's FY26 revenue reached close to thirty-eight thousand crore rupees, built on more than ninety crore orders across the year. Zepto, preparing for what would be India's first standalone quick-commerce IPO, reported roughly twenty-five thousand crore in net retail value and two hundred and ten million orders in a single quarter. Swiggy Instamart grew its gross order value by close to seventy percent year on year — but it's still losing money on the way there.

COMPETITION
And that's before you count the two companies everyone forgets are in this fight. Amazon and Flipkart have both scaled their own quick-commerce arms past five hundred dark stores each — quietly, without the press cycle Zepto and Blinkit get, but with balance sheets neither startup can match. If this becomes a war of attrition instead of a war of speed, the two e-commerce giants are the ones built to survive it longest.

CONTRADICTION
Here's where it gets interesting. In January 2026, Reuters reported that India's government told all three major platforms to stop advertising themselves as "ten-minute" delivery — the exact promise their entire business is built around — over concerns about rider safety and pay. Around the same time, a group representing India's retail distributors asked the country's competition regulator to investigate the same three companies for predatory pricing. Two separate pressures, arriving at the same moment, aimed at the same three companies.

DEEPER EXPLANATION
None of this shows up in a funding announcement. Venture money measures growth. It doesn't measure whether a government is comfortable with how that growth was achieved, or whether the small shopkeepers these platforms are out-competing have enough political weight to slow them down. Quick commerce didn't just disrupt the corner store. In a country where millions of families depend on small retail for their income, it disrupted a constituency — and constituencies eventually show up in regulation.

SECOND REVELATION
Which is what makes Blinkit's profitability the real headline, not a footnote. A company that's already covering its costs can absorb a marketing restriction or a regulatory inquiry far more easily than one still burning venture capital to hold its market share. The quiet transition from "growth story" to "margin story" isn't happening because these companies chose it. It's happening because the ground under them — regulators, retailers, and eventually investors — stopped being willing to fund losses indefinitely.

FINAL PAYOFF
So the next time you open one of these apps and watch the ten-minute countdown start, remember what's actually being timed. It's not just your groceries. It's how long a business built entirely on speed can survive now that speed alone isn't the thing being judged anymore.

India ka Semiconductor Gamble

हिंदी
HOOK

Gujarat mein ek factory bann rahi hai jiski keemat gyarah billion dollar hai — aur ye factory abhi puri tarah taiyar bhi nahi hui, lekin do alag reports already ek-dusre se contradict kar rahi hain ki ye factory kya banayegi.

OPEN QUESTION

Sawaal simple hai: India apna pehla semiconductor fab bana raha hai — lekin kya ye cutting-edge chip technology hai, ya fir ek soch-samajh ke liya gaya safe, purana rasta?

CONTEXT + DATA

Tata Electronics, Taiwan ki company PSMC ke saath milkar, Dholera mein India ka pehla front-end fab bana rahi hai — investment lagbhag 91,000 crore rupaye. Poore India Semiconductor Mission ka approved investment hai 1,65,685 crore, 12 facilities approved, lekin sirf 3 operational — aur wo teeno assembly-packaging plants hain, front-end fab nahi.

CONTRADICTION

PSMC ke chairman ne kaha tha fab 28-nanometer se shuru hoga. Lekin July 2026 mein Business Standard ne Bloomberg ke hawale se likha ki production 90-nanometer se shuru hogi — ek bahut purani, mature technology.

REVELATION + PAYOFF

Ye contradiction hi asli story hai — Tata cutting-edge race mein nahi kood raha, pehle wahi cheez master kar raha hai jo Taiwan ne decades pehle ki thi. Agla saal jab India ka pehla chip banega, yaad rakhiyega — wo duniya ka sabse advanced processor nahi hoga, aur shayad yahi plan ka sabse smart hissa hai.

Quick Commerce Ki Asli Ladai

हिंदी
HOOK

January 2026 mein Reuters ne report kiya ki government ne teeno platforms ko '10-minute delivery' bolna band karne ko kaha — rider safety concerns ki wajah se. Same time pe retailers ke ek group ne inke against predatory pricing ki complaint bhi daali.

PAYOFF

Toh agli baar jab app pe 10-minute ka countdown chale, samajhiye — ye sirf grocery ka time nahi hai. Ye dekhne ka time hai ki speed pe bana business, tab bhi zinda reh sakta hai jab speed hi judge nahi ho rahi.

Anthropic vs OpenAI — Enterprise Ki Chhupi Jung

हिंदी
HOOK
